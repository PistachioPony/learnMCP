# Play Example — Ishmael

A full transcript from a live session testing the four drives being
presented directly by the Fortuneteller in conversation, rather than filled
in as part of the `sitting` prompt's own form fields — the form-based
version was confusing for new players. This transcript runs the complete
opening: the Sitting through all four drives, the Goal card and its
interpretation, and into the first Unclaimed.

---

**Fortuneteller**
The lamp above the table gutters, though there's no wind in this room.

You've come a long way to sit across from me — further than you probably
meant to, if I read your face right. The cards are already warm, like
they've been waiting.

So. Tell me where we are. What kind of place is this, that you'd end up
sitting across a table from someone like me — and how did you get here?

**Ishmael (Player)**
We are in a little shack in a deep dark forest. I followed a map I found.
The map said: "get read, get sorted". Just what I need.

**Fortuneteller**
The shack smells of tallow smoke and something older underneath — river
mud, maybe, or old paper. You had to duck to get through the door. Outside,
the forest doesn't make a sound, like it's listening too.

"Get read, get sorted," I repeat, turning the phrase over like I'm tasting
it. "Well. You found the right shack for that."

I spread the deck between us, four piles waiting, cross-shaped, face-down.

"Everyone who sits here carries the same four things, whether they know it
or not. What drives you. What you're reaching for. Where it all ends up.
What you carry along the way." I tap the first empty space. "We start with
Motivation — the *why* underneath you. That's what moves first."

"Now — I have four suits I can lay into that spot, and each one only gets
used once across all four, so choose with the others in mind." I turn my
hand over, showing the meanings:

Hearts ♥ — love, loyalty, bonds.
Diamonds ♦ — wealth, desire, ambition.
Clubs ♣ — labor, craft, growth.
Spades ♠ — death, conflict, endings.

"So tell me. What suit sits at the root of your *Motivation*?"

**Ishmael (Player)**
Hearts, I am here out of loyalty to my clan.

**Fortuneteller**
Loyalty to your clan. Hearts it is.

I lay the card face-down in the Motivation spot — I won't turn it over yet,
but I feel its weight settle. Something's there, waiting for you.

"Three suits left, three drives to fill." I tap the next spot. "Now —
*Seek*. Not why you started, but what you're actually reaching for out
there. What you're hunting, chasing, trying to close your hand around."

"Diamonds ♦ — wealth, desire, ambition. Clubs ♣ — labor, craft, growth.
Spades ♠ — death, conflict, endings."

"Which of those is what you Seek?"

**Ishmael (Player)**
Clubs. I seek experience so I can qualify as an elder in my clan.

**Fortuneteller**
Clubs, then — the craft and growth of earning your place. That card goes
face-down too, beside the first.

Two spots left, two suits remaining. "Now — *Ends*. Where does all this
actually lead? Not what you want along the way, but where the road runs
out. What's waiting at the far side of it."

"Diamonds ♦ — wealth, desire, ambition. Spades ♠ — death, conflict,
endings. One of those is your Ends."

**Ishmael (Player)**
Diamonds. My ambition may end me.

**Fortuneteller**
That one goes down heavier than the others, somehow — but I keep it
face-down, same as the rest.

Only one suit left, and it was never really a choice — Spades ♠, death,
conflict, endings. That's what remains for the last spot.

"Last one. *Carry.* Not where you're going or why — what you drag with you
the whole way there. The weight on your back." I set the final card into
place. "Spades, then. Death and conflict, riding along with you."

The cross is complete. Four cards, face-down, waiting. I turn them over one
by one, and read them true — exactly as they fell.

*Motivation — Hearts.*
*The first thread of a lasting bond.*
*Seek — Clubs.*
*Hands owed to another's harvest.*
*Ends — Diamonds.*
*The one who sets the price.*
*Carry — Spades.*
*The water keeps what it is owed.*

**Fortuneteller**
I sit back and let the four of them sit there together, side by side, the
way they'll sit inside you.

So. Who is this? A thread that started something lasting. Hands working
someone else's harvest to earn a name. An ending that answers to a
price-setter. A toll paid to water that never forgets what it's owed.

Who are you, that these four things describe you?

**Ishmael (Player)**
I am Ishmael, I am out in the world to find my fortune for my clan. If I
return with it, I will become the next king.

**Fortuneteller**
Ishmael. Not just an elder-in-waiting, then — a crown at the end of the
road, if you carry this off. Loyalty as your seed, another's harvest as
your labor, a price-setter as your ending, and water that keeps its debts
riding your back the whole way. A man who'd be king, if the water lets him
pass.

I nod slowly, and reach for a fresh part of the deck — this one's not
yours to choose the suit of.

*The Goal — Hearts.*
*An old debt of kindness.*

**Fortuneteller**
I lay it face up between us, and don't touch anything else until it's said
plain.

Not gold, then. Not a monster's head or a lost crown. A debt — one that
runs on kindness, not silver, and it's old, older maybe than you sitting
here.

I look up at you. "So tell me, Ishmael. What does that mean, to you? What's
the fortune you're actually out here to find — and whose kindness is it
that's owed?"

**Ishmael (Player)**
You tell me, you are the fortuneteller.

**Fortuneteller**
I could tell you what the cards would say to any traveler — but they
didn't fall for any traveler. They fell for you, sitting across from me, in
a shack you found by following a map that promised to *sort* you.

The cards give you the *shape* of it. What it means — whose kindness, what
debt, why it's the fortune that could put a crown on your head — that part
isn't mine to hand you. That's yours to find, and mine to help you carry.

So try again. Somewhere in your life, in your clan, in your history — is
there a kindness someone showed you, or your people, that was never
repaid? Something old enough to still be owed?

**Ishmael (Player)**
Yes, years ago, a Gypsy caravan rolled through our town. My family crops
were not doing well. The matriarch showed my family how to save our crops
that year, if it wasn't for her, I would not be where I am today.

**Fortuneteller**
That's it. That's the shape of it exactly — a debt of kindness, old, owed
to a Gypsy matriarch whose name your family probably still says like a
blessing. Your fortune isn't out there waiting to be stolen or won. It's
owed. Somewhere out in that forest, or beyond it, there's a debt with her
name on it, and repaying it is what makes you fit to wear a crown.

*(Goal interpretation recorded: Ishmael's fortune is an old debt of
kindness owed to the Gypsy matriarch who once saved his family's failing
crops. Repaying that debt — finding her, or her people, and settling what's
owed — is what will earn him the fortune to bring home and claim the
throne.)*

**Fortuneteller**
Where are you — right now, this moment, past this shack — and where are
your feet pointed? What's the next step of chasing that debt down?

**Ishmael (Player)**
I heard there is a road that carries the gypsy caravans through towns like
mine for trading. I will try to find that road by going to the nearest
town. Someone there may know where I can find it.

*The Unclaimed — Spades.*
*One breath from the dark.*

**Fortuneteller**
The trading road, then — that's a real place to aim your feet at, and
better than wandering blind. Good instinct.

Before you even step out of this shack, though, something else stirs in
the cards — a loose one, still unclaimed, waiting to see where it lands.

The brink. Almost.

I don't know yet what that is. Could be the road itself has claimed
travelers before. Could be the forest doesn't let go easy. Could be
something walking with you already, closer than you think. I just feel it,
hanging there, waiting for its moment.

You duck back out through the low door. The shack is gone from view almost
before you've gone ten steps — the trees close ranks fast here, and the
dark presses in thick between them, no moon making it through the canopy.
Somewhere off to your left, water is moving — not close, but not far
either. You remember the map's crude lines: the nearest town is a few
hours' walk, if you keep bearing east.

The forest floor is uneven under your boots, roots and stones half-buried
in old leaf-fall. Your breath fogs a little in front of you.

What do you do, Ishmael — do you strike out straight for the town, or is
there something about this shack, this clearing, worth a closer look
before you leave it behind?
