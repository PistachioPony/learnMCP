# The Fortuneteller's Hand MCP Server Game — Rules

This is an adaptation of the tabletop roleplaying game called The
Fortuneteller's Hand, by Maria Saha, created to learn the principles of
Model Context Protocol.

## The Sitting

Character creation — the first scene, not setup before
the game.

1. Player decides where the character meets the Fortuneteller.
2. Assign each of the four suits to: **Motivation** (what drives you), **Ends** (what
   finishes you), **Seek** (what you're after), **Carry** (what you bring).
   The suits mean : Hearts - love, loyalty, bonds. Diamonds - wealth, desire, ambition. Clubs - labor, craft, growth. Spades - death, conflict, endings. The fortune teller will then Speak the four phrases. Now the Player will take those phrases and describe the character they add up to.
3. Now the Fortuneteller will deal the Goal — one card, the fate the character is walking
   toward (only after the character exists, not before).
4. The player interprets the goal
5. The character sets out.

## The Turn

Play unfolds in scenes, each one built the same way:

1. **WHERE** — decide where the character is now.
2. A card is flipped from The Unclaimed Deck and
   read cold, before any narration: its phrase, and then the player must interpret the line grounding it to the scene.
3. **The scene** — narrate forward. The player authors their own
   character's words, choices, and actions. Fate keeps three things behind
   its hand — other hearts, hidden things, the turn of luck — and that
   includes Claude's own narration, not only the player's. The instant a
   beat reaches into one of those three, or a player claims an uncertain,
   high-stakes action simply succeeded, anything that needs a yes/no
   answer — stop and ask fate by calling for an omen (see Omens, below). At
   least one omen must be asked before the scene closes.
4. **How a scene is closed** — the scene ends once two things are both true: the
   Unclaimed's grounded material has actually landed in the fiction, and an
   omen's been asked and resolved. Name the close out loud, then return to
   WHERE for the next scene.

The Unclaimed can also be drawn again mid-scene, any time something
genuinely new needs material rather than a yes/no.

## Omens

Ask a real question with real stakes; hope for an outcome. Cast two
ten-sided dice, one light, one dark — never summed, only compared.

- **Doubles** — fate eats the question. No answer. The *hand is called*
  instead — see The Called Hand, below.
- **Otherwise** — the higher die gives direction, light or dark; the gap
  between them gives texture:
  - 1–3: *"...but"* — a complication rides in
  - 4–6: clean
  - 7–9: *"...and"* — better than asked, or worse than feared
  - The losing die's rank gives the complication's plain meaning — rank
    only, never a suit.

State the shape — direction, gap, complication — and stop completely. The
player grounds it in their own words before anything else happens.

## The Called Hand

Doubles trigger it immediately: if you already owe fate from an earlier
defiance (see Debt, below), that comes due first; otherwise a fresh card is
dealt from the Hand. Either way, the landing must intrude — it costs the
scene, and the original question stays unanswered. Claude always narrates
the landing, whether the card is a returning debt or a fresh deal.

If a landed fortune is one the player wants to fight, they call defiance.

## Defiance

Five six-sided dice, up to three rolls (Yahtzee-style), keeping any subset
between rolls. The more rolls taken, the more debt accrued. The final
pattern earns the player a chance to change the *how*, *when*, or *whom* of
the fate that falls:

- Three of a kind — turn the blade: the *how* changes, target and moment
  stay.
- Small straight — stay the hand: the *when*, delayed one full scene.
- Full house — take the cup: the *whom*, a different head, the player's own
  included.
- Five of a kind — seize the pen: full authorship of the landing, within the
  card. This means the player gets the voice back and can narrate the
  scene as they choose.

## Debt

Fate deals the player one debt card per roll taken, whether or not a
pattern was ever bought. Each one comes due later — it must be played into
the narration at the next Called Hand.

## Table conventions

- **"Read it truer"** — at any point, if a narration betrays a card's
  domain, magnitude, or phrase, say so. No defending the read — just narrate
  it again, truer.
- **`((double parens))`** — anything inside double parentheses is an aside
  to Claude-the-collaborator, not in-fiction dialogue or action.
