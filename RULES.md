# learnMCP — Rules

The Fortuneteller's Hand, adapted for one player with Claude as the
fortuneteller and the game's GM. Where this departs from the original game,
it's a deliberate solo adaptation, not a correction — see the `fortunteller`
repo for canon.

## The Sitting

Character creation, played in character — the first scene, not setup before
the game.

1. Decide together where the character meets the Fortuneteller.
2. Assign each of the four suits to a cross position — the one choice made
   about fate itself: **Motivation** (what drives you), **Ends** (what
   finishes you), **Seek** (what you're after), **Carry** (what you bring).
   Draw one card blind from each chosen suit. Speak the four phrases, then
   describe the character they add up to.
3. Deal the Goal — one card, the shared fate the character is walking
   toward — only after the character exists, not before.
4. The character sets out.

## The Turn

Play unfolds in scenes, each one built the same way:

1. **WHERE** — decide where the character is now.
2. **The Unclaimed** — a card is flipped from the Fortuneteller's Hand and
   read cold, before any narration: its phrase, then one line grounding how
   it could come true here.
3. **The scene** — narrate forward. The player authors their own
   character's words, choices, and actions. Fate keeps three things behind
   its hand — other hearts, hidden things, the turn of luck — and that
   includes Claude's own narration, not only the player's. The instant a
   beat reaches into one of those three, or a player claims an uncertain,
   high-stakes action simply succeeded, stop and ask fate. At least one
   omen must be asked before the scene closes.
4. **Close** — the scene ends once two things are both true: the
   Unclaimed's grounded material has actually landed in the fiction, and an
   omen's been asked and resolved. Name the close out loud, then return to
   WHERE for the next scene.

The Unclaimed can also be drawn again mid-scene, any time something
genuinely new needs material rather than a yes/no.

(LENS — canon's per-turn cross-position throughline — is deliberately left
out for now. Revisit if play keeps wanting it.)

## Omens

Ask a real question with real stakes; hope for an outcome. Cast two
ten-sided dice, one light, one dark — never summed, only compared.

- **Doubles** — fate eats the question. No answer. The hand is called
  instead.
- **Otherwise** — the higher die gives direction, light or dark; the gap
  between them gives texture:
  - 1–3: *"...but"* — a complication rides in
  - 4–6: clean
  - 7–9: *"...and"* — better than asked, or worse than feared
  - The losing die's rank gives the complication's plain meaning — rank
    only, never a suit.

State the shape — direction, gap, complication — and stop completely. The
player grounds it in their own words before anything else happens.

## The Called Hand

Doubles trigger it immediately. If any debt is outstanding, the table's
oldest debt card comes due; otherwise a fresh card is dealt from the Hand.
Either way, the landing must intrude — it costs the scene, and the original
question stays unanswered. Claude always narrates the landing, whether the
card is a returning debt or a fresh deal.

If a landed fortune is one the player wants to fight, they call defiance.

## Defiance

Five six-sided dice, up to three rolls, keeping any subset between rolls.
What the final pattern earns:

- Three of a kind — turn the blade: the *how* changes, target and moment
  stay
- Small straight — stay the hand: the *when*, delayed one full scene
- Full house — take the cup: the *whom*, a different head, the player's own
  included
- Five of a kind — seize the pen: full authorship of the landing, within the
  card

Resolving deals one debt card per roll taken, whether or not a pattern was
ever bought.

## The Unclaimed, mechanically

Drawn from the Fortuneteller's Hand — the same deck the called hand deals
from. Once drawn, a card doesn't go back into the live deck; it moves to the
Told. If the Hand deck ever runs dry, the Told reshuffles back into a fresh
deck. A card is never redrawn while others are still unseen — that's what
keeps fate's voice finite and committed, not just another die to roll.

## Table conventions

- **"Read it truer"** — at any point, if a narration betrays a card's
  domain, magnitude, or phrase, say so. No defending the read — just narrate
  it again, truer.
- **`((double parens))`** — anything inside double parentheses is an aside
  to Claude-the-collaborator, not in-fiction dialogue or action.
